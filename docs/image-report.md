# Image report — what we recovered from the old site, and what we still need

**Prepared for:** Sato Engineering & Infrastructure Limited
**Date:** 24 September 2026
**Purpose:** to agree which existing photographs can be used on the new website, and to commission
the new photography needed before the letters go out to the oil and gas companies.

---

## In one page

We recovered **111 photographs** from the current website (satoengineering.com). **74 are good
enough to use**, and they are already prepared and placed on the new site. The remaining 37 are
duplicates, page decoration, or too small to be worth keeping.

The problem is not quantity. It is **age and size**.

- Most of the usable pictures were taken in **2012 or earlier**, on cameras of that era. **Half of
  them are under 700 pixels wide** — roughly the size of a postage stamp on a modern laptop screen.
  They can be shown small, but they fall apart if enlarged.
- Only **11 photographs** are large enough to fill the top of a page. Four of those eleven show
  identifiable people, one is a photograph of an award plaque, and two are tight interior shots
  that do not work as an establishing image. That leaves **4 usable page-top photographs** for the
  whole site.
- The eight page-top banners on the current site are **stock photographs** of foreign construction
  sites with a model in a Sato hard hat pasted on top. They are not Sato's work and we have not
  carried any of them over. This matters: a procurement officer who reverse-image-searches one of
  them will find it on a stock library, and that is the opposite of the impression we are trying
  to make.
- There are **no photographs of any member of staff** on the current site. The leadership page has
  sixteen names and no faces.

So the site will launch looking credible, but thin. **The single highest-value thing you can do is
send a phone round three or four active or completed sites and photograph them properly.** A
current-model phone already takes pictures four times larger than almost anything we recovered.

This is the list, in priority order. A recent phone held horizontally, in the morning or late
afternoon, is enough — no photographer needed. Please do not crop or filter anything; send the
original files.

**1. Three or four wide establishing shots, for the top of each page.** No people in frame. Stand
back far enough to show the whole thing against sky.

   - A completed road, shot down the centre line

   - **An oil and gas or energy site, if you have one.** We currently have nothing for this sector,
     and it is the sector the letters target.

**2. Portraits of the leadership team.** Sixteen names on the leadership page, no faces. Plain
wall, daylight from a window, head and shoulders, same background for everyone. This is the
cheapest credibility we can buy and it takes one afternoon.

**3. Plant and equipment.** We have eight usable equipment photographs out of forty-nine items on
your equipment schedule. Photographs of the yard, and of the larger items with Sato livery
visible, would carry the capability claim far better than a list. Note also that the backhoe

send a replacement.

**4. The three projects with no pictures at all:** Ogungbade river dredging, Academic Building
Complex Block B, and the Ogun State affordable housing scheme. Even one photograph each.

**5. Anything from after 2019.** The newest photograph on the current site is from October 2019.
Seven years of work is unrecorded. If there are site photographs on anyone's phone or in a project
file, they are almost certainly better than what we are working with.

**6. Certificates and registrations.** Clean scans of the CAC certificate, COREN registration and
any HSE certification. These are not decoration — they are the documents a procurement officer is
checking for.

## Appendix — technical notes

**Where things live**

- `raw-assets/old-site/` — the 111 originals, exactly as downloaded. **Not committed** (see
  `.gitignore`); it is a working directory, reproducible from the manifest.
- `raw-assets/manifest.json` — every image with its source URL, the page it appeared on, its
  caption, and its pixel dimensions.
- `scripts/image-map.json` — the curated selection: which original becomes which web image, and
  the alt text for each.
- `scripts/optimise-images.mjs` — regenerates `public/images/` from the originals. Run with
  `npm run images`. Exits cleanly if `raw-assets/` is absent, since the generated images are
  committed and the site builds from those.

**What the optimiser produces**

Each selected image becomes two WebP files: one capped at 1920px wide and one capped at 800px.
Neither is ever upscaled, so both paths always exist and a component can build a `srcset` without
branching. 148 files, 12.3 MB total; the 800px set — which is what a phone on a slow connection
downloads — averages 52 KB per image.

**Where they are wired in**

- `content/projects.json` — 13 of 16 projects now carry `images[]`, with real width and height
  so pages do not shift as images load.
- `content/services.json` — three of four divisions have a lead image. Energy has none.
- `content/equipment.json` — 8 of 49 items have a photograph. `Equipment.image` is a new optional
  field on the type.
- `content/site.json` — `heroImages[]` holds the four cleared page-top candidates. `Site.heroImages`
  is a new field on the type.
- `content/team.json` — unchanged. All 16 `photo` fields remain `null`; the old site published none.

**Deliberately not carried over**

The eight 1260×400 page banners, all stock composites; a BMP file saved with a `.jpg` extension
that no image library will open; and ten byte-identical duplicates uploaded twice under different
names.
