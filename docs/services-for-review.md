## What you are approving, and what you are not

These drafts describe **what each service offers**. Except where a section is
explicitly attributed to the partner team, they are not claims that Sato has
already delivered that work.

The question for each service is simply: **can Sato deliver this, and do you
want it offered?** Strike anything you are not comfortable standing behind in
front of an oil company's vendor-verification team — that is exactly who will
read these pages.

### Two different kinds of borrowed material

This matters, because the two are handled in opposite ways.

**Services 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6** were drafted from partner reference sites, and
only the *description of the capability* was taken. No project, client,
certification, staff number, year, location, statistic or award of theirs
appears anywhere. Sato does not claim another company's track record. Two of
them say in their own body text that Sato has no completed projects in that
area yet — that is deliberate, and worth keeping: it is the sentence that
stops the rest of the page reading as a claim.

**Service 4, Oil & Gas, is different.** It does carry the partner team's
projects, case study and awards, because the offer itself is joint and the
capability is not credible without them. Everything borrowed sits inside a
section headed "Experience of our partner team" and is attributed on the
page. Section 4 below sets out exactly how, and that separation is the main
thing to check when you read it.

### How the two track records are kept apart

This is the most important thing to check on the page, so it is worth
stating plainly.

The source deck presents the oil and gas capability of **Sato together with
its technical partner**, and its project list is titled "Past Projects with
Partners". The headline figures — projects, patents, publications, countries
— describe the partner team's experience, not Sato's delivery history.

The page is built so a reader cannot mistake one for the other:

- **Capabilities and solutions** are written as what Sato offers ("We
  provide…", "We source and procure…"). They are claims about the service.
- **Every past project, figure, patent and award** sits inside a section
  headed **"Experience of our partner team"**, introduced as "Our technical
  partners have delivered…", and the case study carries its own line: "A
  project delivered by our partner team."
- **None of it appears on the Projects page**, in Sato's project counts, or
  in the site's structured data.

An oil company will verify these projects. If ADNOC's work appeared as
Sato's own, the first verification call would end the conversation. Please
read the attribution wording and tell us if it goes too far or not far
enough.

### Headline figures — nothing is published yet

The source gives numbers that contradict each other:

| Figure | Values found |
|---|---|
| Projects | 170, 250+, 280+ |
| Patents | 6, 8 |
| Countries | 13, 15 |
| Experience | 25+ years, "over 200 years combined" |
| Publications | 150+ |

A figures strip is built and wired to `partner.figures` in
`content/services.json`, but the field is **empty and the strip is hidden**.
Publishing a number that a reader can find contradicted elsewhere costs more
credibility than publishing none. Send one agreed set and they appear.

### Deliberately left out

Two claims from the source are **not on the page**. They can be added if you
want them, but the recommendation is to leave them out:

- **"100% on budget / on schedule / exceeding expectations."** An absolute
  with no scope, period or auditor behind it. Procurement teams discount
  these on sight, and it invites the question of what happens on the first
  project that slips.
- **"+10–200% profit, 1000% ROI."** A return figure that depends entirely on
  the client's own cost base. It reads as a sales claim rather than an
  engineering one.

**Say the word and either goes back in.**
